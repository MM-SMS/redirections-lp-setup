"It's better than a hair transplant!" How I saved my hair despite genetics and age

She had spent 25 years fixing other people's hair. Then, at 46, her own began to fall out — and nothing in the salon could stop it. A hair stylist explains what finally did.

Hello! My name is Emma Cooper, I am 46 years old, and I have been a hair stylist for most of my life. Despite all my knowledge and experience, I faced serious problems with my own hair. Popular products turned out to be useless, and I had to look for another way. Today I want to tell you how I completely restored — and even significantly improved — the condition of my hair in a short time, without dangerous procedures or expensive drugs.

As a stylist, I have always prided myself on my hair care skills, but that did not save me from disaster after 40. At first my hair began to thin gradually. I ignored it, thinking the problem would go away on its own, and kept using regular hair care products.

After two or three months the loss became more noticeable. My hair got drier and I started going gray earlier than my friends. I tried other professional products and supplements — no improvement. The worst part was that the hair loss became obvious to others. As a stylist, it was hard to accept that I couldn't fix my own hair. Some days I just wanted to stay home and avoid my clients.

Of course, I didn't accept it at first. I am only 46, I have worked with hair all my life — and now this. I knew there were risks at my age, but I hoped I would never face it. I take care of my hair, I use quality cosmetics, I eat well. And I still didn't avoid the problem.

But work is work. I started wearing a scarf or putting my hair in a bun so no one could see its condition, while I searched for a way to stop the loss.

"A trichologist will not help you…"

In despair, I went to a trichologist. He said I had first-stage alopecia related to age and hormonal changes, and prescribed a medicated shampoo for two weeks plus vitamins. Despite all my efforts, the hair loss only increased. The doctor said that if it got worse I would need laser hair therapy. That scared me the most.

Did you know?

Alopecia in women doesn't always mean bald spots

In women it often starts as increased shedding plus dry, brittle, dull hair. Around 40% of American women notice visible thinning by age 50 — and most mistake the early signs for "bad hair days".

  • Hair falls out in larger amounts when washing or brushing
  • The part line looks wider than it used to
  • Hair has lost its shine and breaks easily
  • Gray hair appears earlier than expected

At home I read everything I could about alopecia in women. It develops a little differently in us than in men. If your hair has become brittle and dry, has lost its shine and is falling out in greater amounts, these are the first signs. It is possible to stop it — and I will tell you how.

— What a nightmare. I'm so sorry…

I was shocked. I read about every existing procedure and its risks — from simply not working to complete hair loss — and realized I needed a safer method.
